20 July 2026

Results!

This term I studied two modules: Object-Oriented Programming (Java) and UX & Interaction Design. Both finished with a three-hour timed exam.

I knew the Java exam was always going to be the bigger challenge, and it certainly was. Fortunately, my UX coursework marks over the year — 77%, 97%, 89% and 99% — gave me a healthy cushion if the exam turned into a disaster.

As it happened, both exams were much tougher than I expected. I did not manage to finish every question on either paper, but I did enough to secure solid passes in both modules.

For some reason, I was absolutely racked with anxiety while waiting for the results. The thought of having to resit both exams in September, just as my final two modules were starting, played on my mind far more than it probably should have.

Unfortunately, the Open University also suffered technical problems on results day. Ironically, they were exactly the sort of issues we had covered in the Systems Planning module.

As I understand it, the results portal still relies on legacy on-premises infrastructure rather than cloud-based auto-scaling. Whatever the exact cause, it clearly could not cope with the surge in demand, leaving many students, myself included, locked out by 502 and 504 Gateway Error messages for hours.

One positive to come out of the experience was a much better appreciation of what students at school must feel while waiting for exam results. Even knowing I had probably done enough to scrape through on both papers, the uncertainty was exhausting.

...really need to start planning my capstone project.

22 June 2026

Feed the Birds

I have been following the RSPB's advice on summer feeding for a while now: more here.

Essentially, the advice is to pause feeding seeds and peanuts between 1 May and 31 October to help prevent the spread of diseases such as trichomonosis. I am just providing fat balls and fresh water.

The garden seems busier than ever. I wonder whether many people have stopped feeding altogether during the summer.

Robins, blackbirds, sparrows, blue tits, woodpigeons and, recently, magpies have all been visiting.

The blue tits and sparrows have now vacated the boxes with their fledglings. Good stuff.
